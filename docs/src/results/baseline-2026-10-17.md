# The baseline of 2026-10-17

The third baseline of linlog's engines, taken by `bench/baseline.sh` on
2026-10-17 from 19:32 for 2 h 58 min, at commit `ed74387d9bf3`, the
engines of the first release, in a release build on an Intel Core Ultra
X9 388H (16 cores, 62 GB). The sequential runs went in four streams at
once, each pinned to a performance core of its own; a time is the
median of up to three runs. Every proof of the baseline passed the
checker. The tables of every run are
[bench/RESULTS.md](https://github.com/linlog-prover/linlog/blob/main/bench/RESULTS.md),
the rows `bench/results/2026-10-17/`, and the three baselines compared
[bench/COMPARISON.md](https://github.com/linlog-prover/linlog/blob/main/bench/COMPARISON.md),
from which the tables below are taken.

**The families' time against their size.** The wall time of every
generated family's instances under the engine the command chooses for
it, the median of the runs of `families.csv` above (one thread, 300 s),
both axes logarithmic, a line per engine; a size where most runs of
some instance ended undecided, at the time limit or a bound, is a
hollow marker on the limit's line. The partitions, `qbf` and `mix`
rise ever more steeply with their size; `wide-m*`, `chain` and
`additive` follow nearly straight lines on the two logarithmic axes, a
power of the size; `counter` and `counter-over`, Petri nets the Horn
engine searches by their markings, stay at tens of microseconds; and
`growing` ends at the copy bound or the recursion limit at every
size.

**The focused engine against the net engine.** The same measure for the
families of `engines.csv`, where the baseline forces each engine in
turn (one thread, 60 s, the same machine and run): the focused engine
decides every instance, where the net engine leaves `partition-yes`,
`partition-no` and `3-partition-mll-no` undecided past their smallest
sizes; on the `wide` families, two of which the command gives to the
net engine, the two grow alike, the focused engine a nearly constant
factor ahead. The net engine refuses `additive`, which has no proof
nets.

pass. The pass under the command's default (no copy bound, one thread
for 0.1 s and then a pool of the other fifteen beside it, 2 s) decides
4 024 of the 4 495 problems: 3 069 nets and 955 others.
