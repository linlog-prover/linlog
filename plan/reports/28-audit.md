# Step 28, stage 1: the audit

One session (`step-28b`, Opus 5.5 at `high`) on 2026-10-09, unattended
and supervised by the planning session. It wrote the rubric
(`plan/notes/audit-rubric.md`), made what a machine can check a check of
`nix flake check`, and ran the audit as one workflow of 81 agents on
three models. The step's checklist is in
`plan/reports/28-audit-and-refactor.md`; every finding, with its
evidence, its proposed fix and both examiners' verdicts, is in
`plan/reports/28-audit-findings.json`, which a fix session searches by
its identifier (`F1` … `F226`).

  both examiners, 18 by one of the two ("contested", with the

## How the audit ran

One workflow, `wf_715f365e-ce0`, started at 03:52 on 2026-10-09 and
ended at 06:02 (2 h 10 min). Its script is kept in the session's directory
(`step28-audit-wf_715f365e-ce0.js` under the session's `workflows/scripts/`);
its result, every finding with its examiners' verdicts, is
`plan/reports/28-audit-findings.json`.

- **Round one, independent.** Seventeen reviewers, one lens each, every
  one in a fresh context with the same brief: the rubric
  (`plan/notes/audit-rubric.md`), the register, stage 0's evidence (the
  mutants that survive, the fuzz find, the journeys), the leftovers the
  earlier steps named, and the rules of `plan/conduct.md` for any
  program it ran (cores 6 to 15, a scope of 4 GB, ten minutes, two
  threads at most, a shared prebuilt target directory). The ten lenses
  of the prompt, with the models and efforts it names; where a lens's
  scope was too large for one context to read in full it was split by
  area, so that each reviewer could read its part rather than sample it:

  | lens | reviewers | model, effort |
  |---|---|---|
  | soundness of the checker, proof terms, nets and their integers | `sound-check` | Fable 5.1, high |
  | soundness of every refutation | `sound-focus` (the focused and net engines, the additive path, the front door), `sound-horn` (the Horn engine, the ordinary layer) | Fable 5.1, high |
  | readiness for the later steps | `ready-a` (wire forms, bounds, wasm, errors, options, command, harness, docs), `ready-b` (data model, engine interface, proof term, exports) | Fable 5.1, high |
  | the API, data model and wire forms | `api-surface` (Rust API Guidelines), `api-wire` (the AIPs, the errors) | Opus 5.5, xhigh |
  | performance | `perf` | Opus 5.5, high |
  | untrusted input and resource bounds | `input` | Opus 5.5, high |
  | idiom, structure, reuse | `idiom-search`, `idiom-core`, `idiom-outer` (command, harness, flake, scripts) | Opus 5.5, high |
  | doc comments, names, comments | `docs-a` (search, proofs), `docs-b` (the rest, the crate docs, the help texts) | Opus 5.5, medium |
  | tests | `tests-core`, `tests-outer` | Sonnet 5.5, high |
  | coherence of the documents | `coherence` | Sonnet 5.5, high |

- **Merging.** Duplicates by code (the same file, line and main
  criterion), then near-duplicates by one agent (Opus 5.5, high), which
  named groups that one fix removes; the code merged them, keeping every
  place and every lens.
- **Round two, cross-examination.** Every merged finding went to two
  examiners of models other than the one that raised it (a finding of
  Opus to Fable and Sonnet, of Fable to Opus and Sonnet, of Sonnet to
  Fable and Opus), in batches of at most thirteen, each examiner told to refute
  with the code in hand and to give the severity it would give. A
  finding falls when both refute it; one refutation leaves it standing
  as "contested", with the refutation quoted beside it below.
- **The completeness critic** (Fable 5.1, high) compared the lenses'
  scopes and their own lists of what they had not read with the tree,
  the leftovers and the mutant lists, and named gaps, each for a lens,
  and findings that rest on no evidence. Each gap was read by an agent
  of that lens's model and effort, told the findings already standing;
  their findings were merged and cross-examined as above; each finding
  without evidence went to one more examiner (Opus 5.5, high), and fell
  if that one refuted it. Rounds stop when one adds no standing finding,
  two at most. Round one gave 284 findings, merged by code to 253 and
  by the merging agent to 190, of which 184 stood. The first critic
  named 8 gaps and 2 findings without evidence; its gap round added 19
  findings, all standing. The second named 8 gaps and 3 such findings
  (two of them again); its round gave 24, 23 of them new, all standing.
  Every recheck held its finding. The rounds stopped there, at the
  prompt's limit of two, although the second still added findings.
- **What a bound dropped**: nothing. No agent failed or returned
  nothing (the two merges of the gap rounds returned no groups, since
  their findings had no near-duplicates), no finding went unexamined,
  and every finding had two examiners.

## Decided unattended

- **Seventeen reviewers for the ten lenses.** Where a lens's scope was
  more than one context reads in full (the soundness of refutations, the
  readiness, the API, idiom, the doc comments, the tests), it was split
  by area, each part on the lens's model and effort, so that every
  reviewer read its part rather than sampled it. Set aside: one reviewer
  per lens, which the first lens reports showed would have left most of
  the code unread.
- **Cross-examination in batches** of at most thirteen findings per
  examiner, each examiner of a model other than the finding's author:
  two agents per finding would have been 460; batches kept it to 41.
  A finding raised by two models went to the third and to an Opus
  examiner of another lens.
- **A contested finding stands**, as the prompt's rule says (it falls
  when both examiners refute it); its refutation is printed beside it so
  the fix session judges it again.
- **A finding the critic called unsupported** went to one more examiner
  (Opus 5.5 at `high`) and would have fallen on its refutation; all
  three held.
- **81 agents**, where the prompt names "up to about sixty": 17
  reviewers, 3 merges, 41 examiners, 2 critics, 16 gap readers, 2
  rechecks. The rounds the prompt asks for, on 232 findings, needed
  them; the gap rounds stopped at the two the prompt allows.
- **The machine checks keep a backlog** rather than fixing code in this
  stage: the lints chosen that fire today are listed at `allow` in
  `Cargo.toml` under a comment that says so, and each fix area turns one
  on with the commit that clears its last site. Set aside: fixing the
  655 sites now (stage 3's work, and not reviewed as such), and leaving
  the chosen lints out of the configuration (nothing would keep the
  zero-site ones at zero).
- **typos and cargo-shear** for the spelling and the unused
  dependencies, from nixpkgs at the locked revision; set aside codespell
  (Python, more false positives on identifiers), cargo-udeps (nightly)
  and cargo-machete (misses a dependency used under a feature).
- **`unreachable_pub` stays a command of `core.md`** rather than a
  workspace lint: the harness, a binary, has 26 harmless sites.
- **`.claude/` is exempt from the plan grep**: its rules and prompts
  describe the sessions by design; the coherence lens's point that some
  rules files carry history is T4 above.
- **The area of each finding** is the reviewer's, as the fix session of
  that area will take it; a finding whose fix spans two areas is in the
  one that owns its file.
- **A prebuilt target directory for the reviewers** (`target/audit`), so
  that the agents' runs shared one build instead of each starting one.
  One reviewer's probe of `interact` wrote a file `latex` into the
  repository root (the behaviour of F162); it was removed before any
  commit, and a watch on the tree for the rest of the run found nothing
  else.
- Claude Code's harness marked two agents' outputs (the second critic,
  and the second-round reader of the hooks) as matching an
  instruction-shaped pattern: both quote `.claude/settings.json`, which
  they reviewed. Nothing in them was acted on as an instruction.
